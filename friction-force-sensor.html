What to notice

The peak, then the plateau

The force climbs while the block sits still, because static friction matches the pull. Right when the block breaks free the force drops to a lower, steady value. That steady value is kinetic friction.

Contact area

Flip the block onto its small end. The weight is the same, so the normal force is the same. It now presses on a smaller area, which squeezes the microscopic bumps together harder. The two effects cancel, so friction barely changes.

Pull speed

Pull slowly, then quickly. At high speed the force builds up faster, so the block breaks free sooner, but it breaks free at the same peak force and slides at the same plateau. Neither static nor kinetic friction depends on how fast you pull.
